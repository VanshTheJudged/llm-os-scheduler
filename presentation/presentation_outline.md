## Slide 1: OS-Inspired Scheduling for LLM Inference Requests

## Slide 2: Under FCFS, short requests wait behind long ones

- Service time grows with output length (one token per model step)
- vLLM admits requests FCFS (Kwon et al., SOSP 2023)
- Worked example (EXP1): mean JCT 6.75 s under FCFS vs 6.00 s under SJF; same finish time 11 s
- Cost: the longest waiting request (R1) waits 7 s instead of 4 s

## Slide 3: Research question and objectives

- RQ: can lightweight output-length prediction plus OS-style scheduling improve completion time and responsiveness vs FCFS without unacceptable fairness or overhead costs?
- Objectives: predict at admission; order with OS policies; measure against FCFS on identical workloads
- Criteria: TTFT, mean/median/P95/P99 JCT, throughput, waiting by length category, scheduling overhead
- Scope: discrete-event simulation, single slot, 7 experiments, 10 paired seeds

## Slide 4: Where this project sits in prior work

- OS concepts: FCFS, SJF, MLFQ, aging
- Orca (OSDI 2022): iteration-level scheduling
- vLLM (SOSP 2023): PagedAttention; FCFS; preempts latest arrivals first
- FastServe (NSDI 2026): token-level preemption, skip-join MLFQ (planned here)
- S³ (NeurIPS 2023): learned length prediction for memory/throughput
- LTR (NeurIPS 2024): learn to rank lengths, approximates SJF
- Clairvoyant (arXiv 2026): predictive SJF for serial backends, closest setting
- Contribution: validated, reproducible evaluation of established policies, not a new algorithm

## Slide 7: Three policies behind one interface

- FCFS: earliest arrival first; baseline
- Predictive SJF: smallest predicted output first; non-preemptive
- Aging (extension): predicted − a·wait ≡ predicted + a·arrival; a=0 is SJF, a→∞ is FCFS
- MLFQ: planned (needs preemption)
- Heuristic: prior[task] × √(prompt/1024) for summaries × 2 if detail requested, capped at max_tokens

## Slide 8: Paired experiments on a validated simulator

- 3 synthetic mixes; Poisson arrivals at utilisation ρ; 10 paired seeds × 5,000 requests
- Unit of analysis = seed; 95% t-intervals on per-seed change vs FCFS
- Assumed service: prefill 10 ms + 0.2 ms/token, decode 25 ms/token
- Validation: Lindley max gap 1.3e-9 s; P-K/Cobham mean abs error 2.2% (27 cells); deterministic; 59 passed in 7.59s

## Slide 9: Predictive SJF cuts mean JCT by 45% at 80% load

- Chart: mean JCT vs load (FCFS, SJF-heuristic, SJF-oracle reference), mixed workload, 10 seeds
- At ρ = 0.8: mean JCT −44.6% [43.0, 46.1], 10/10 seeds
- Mean TTFT −52.5%; P95 JCT −40.6%
- Throughput unchanged (work-conserving policies)
- Reduction grows with load: 17.6% at ρ = 0.5 to 64.9% at ρ = 0.95

## Slide 11: Long requests pay for the gain

- Mean wait by category (s), FCFS / SJF-heuristic / SJF-oracle: short 85 / 11 / 9.7; long 86.3 / 118.4 / 95.9
- Per-seed max wait of long requests: 413 / 1427 / 2710 s
- P99 JCT with heuristic worse than FCFS at ρ ≥ 0.9: -40.5%, -92.2%
- 60.8 requests/seed exceed FCFS's worst wait (4.7 medium)
- Slowdown of short requests: FCFS 113.3× vs SJF 14.3×

## Slide 12: Extension: aging trades mean JCT for worst-case wait

- Aging rates a ∈ {0 (SJF), 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 30, 100, ∞ (FCFS)} tokens/s; mixed workload, ρ = 0.9
- Mean-JCT gain vs FCFS: 57.7, 57.4, 55.5, 48.5, 41.2, 30.7, 18.3, 7.0, 2.3, 0.0%
- Max wait (s): 1432, 1266, 898, 644, 562, 506, 451, 430, 433, 435
- a = 1 keeps 96% of SJF's gain and cuts its worst-case wait by 37%
- No tested rate reaches FCFS's worst case while keeping most of the gain

## Slide 13: What these results do not show

- Simulator ≠ GPU server: one slot, no batching, assumed timing; gains likely overstated for batched serving
- Synthetic workload: no real prompts; heuristic shares the generator's features (optimistic)
- Missing mechanisms: no preemption, KV-cache, MLFQ; preemption-overhead question unanswered
- Statistics: 10 seeds; tail metrics noisy; two post-hoc analyses labelled as such

## Slide 15: Conclusion

- Ordering by a simple length prediction cut mean JCT 17.6%–64.9% (ρ 0.5–0.95) and TTFT 52.5% at ρ = 0.8
- The gain follows ranking quality; uninformative predictions give none
- Long requests pay: worst-case waits ~3–9× FCFS; aging trades, does not remove, the cost
- All results simulated (single-slot M/G/1); next: validate on a batched GPU server
